# Contingency Plans: When Things Go Wrong

Every plan B for the trip, decided now while calm — not at the gate
while panicking. Rule zero: travel insurance + passport copies in
phone + paper copy in a different bag cover 90% of disasters.

## Missed S8/S9 in Hanau (>06:30, LH454 at 10:10)

- 06:30–07:00: take the next S-Bahn anyway — you have ~2 h buffer
  built in; skip the gate-breakfast block to recover 30 min.
- Track works (strike/closure): regional RE to Frankfurt Hbf, then
  S8/S9 to the airport; or taxi Hanau → FRA (~€80–100, ~35 min).
  Pre-save a taxi number the night before.
- Cutoff: if not at FRA by 08:30, call Lufthansa rebooking line
  immediately (number in booking email) — same-day SFO via Munich
  or Zurich is the usual rescue routing.

## Missed LH454 / cancelled flight

- At the counter: ask for rebooking to SFO same day (via MUC/ZRH
  on Lufthansa/Swiss/United) — Star Alliance gives options.
- EU261: 11.5 h long-haul cancellation = up to €600 compensation
  plus duty of care (meals, hotel). Get the reason in writing.
- If stranded overnight in Frankfurt: airport hotels (Sheraton/
  Hilton Garden Inn walkable in T1 area), keep all receipts.
- Shift the whole itinerary +1 day: CHM is closed Mon–Tue, so a
  missed Wednesday start means reordering days, not cancelling —
  see [13-timeline-days.md](13-timeline-days.md) buffers.

## CBP secondary / long immigration (>1 h)

- Normal, not personal: stay polite, answer only what's asked,
  show return ticket + hotel address without being asked twice.
- If you miss the planned BART: nothing breaks — BART/Caltrain run
  all day. Text the hotel about late check-in.
- Absolute cutoff: Menlo Park arrival after ~18:00 → skip the
  Like sign today, do it Day 1 morning instead (sign is free and
  always there; Bedwell sunset moves to Day 3 evening).

## Lost passport in the US

- Paper copy + phone photo = identity proof for the police report
  (file one — needed for the replacement).
- German Consulate General San Francisco (working hours, appointment
  by phone/email) issues a temporary travel document; carry 2
  passport photos... or take new ones at any CVS/Walgreens.
- ESTA is tied to the passport number — a replacement document means
  calling the airline + CBP guidance before the return flight.
  Prevention beats all of this: hotel safe, carry the copy.

## Lost/dead phone

- Paper backup sheet (carry always): hotel address + phone, tour
  pickup details, flight numbers, consulate address, insurance number.
- Caltrain/BART work with a physical Clipper card (buy at SFO
  machines — another reason to buy Clipper, not phone-only tap).
- Any hotel business center / library gives you email + maps
  printouts. The trip docs exist as files — everything recoverable.

## Rain day (Bay Area, rare but real)

- Outdoor blocks (Bedwell sunset, Stanford Dish, HP garage) slide;
  indoor blocks expand: CHM gets a full day, add the Intel Museum
  (weekday), Cantor Arts Center at Stanford, Apple Park VC.
- Pack the windbreaker regardless — marine layer mornings happen
  even in September/October.

## CHM unexpectedly closed / tour cancelled

- CHM closed → swap Day 2/3 order (Google + Stanford stay, museum
  moves to the next open day: Wed–Sun only).
- Tour cancelled → self-guided fallback: Caltrain day pass,
  Google Visitor Experience + Stanford + Apple Park VC by rideshare
  (~$60–80 total) covers 80% of the tour's value.

## Health: jet lag wins / twisted ankle / sick

- Jet-lag emergency: 20-min nap before 15:00 local max, then sunlight
  + walking. Melatonin 0.5–1 mg at local bedtime (buy at home).
- Ankle/knee: rideshare replaces all walking blocks; museums all
  have benches/cafes — CHM and Apple Park VC are fully accessible.
- Sick: hotel day, fluids, US pharmacy (CVS) for basics; insurance
  card photo in phone; ER only for real emergencies ($$$).

## Money emergency

- Two cards, two places (wallet + hotel safe). Schwab/DKB-style
  no-fee cards avoid the 1.75–2% foreign fee bleed.
- ~$100 cash backup in the safe for card-outage days.
- Western Union / MoneyGram exists at US check-cashing stores as
  the last resort — know it, hopefully never use it.
